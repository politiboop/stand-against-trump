# Not the Same: 1986 to Now

Draft, September 29, 2026. The argument against "both sides are bad," built on sources that were checked before it
was written. The short version is at the bottom. Claims from earlier drafts that were written from memory and never
sourced are listed separately under "Not yet sourced"; do not use them until they are.

You are right about plenty. Both parties take the money. Obama removed people at a higher rate than any president before him and bombed Libya without asking Congress [1][2]. A majority of Senate Democrats, 29 of 50, voted for the Iraq war [3]. Clinton signed the crime bill and the deregulation that helped set up 2008 [NEEDS SOURCE]. The mainstream press misjudged the laptop and paid the Covington family [NEEDS SOURCE]. All true, and none of it makes the two parties the same. Here is what the record says when you ask that question directly.

**Money.** In forty years the deficit turned toward balance twice, and both times the tool was a tax increase: the 1990 deal that cost the elder Bush his job, and the 1993 act that passed without a single Republican vote and needed the vice president to break the tie [4][5]. The surpluses of 1998 to 2001 followed [6]. Every large deficit-widening tax law of the era was Republican, and the newest one is scored at 3.4 trillion dollars over ten years [7]. Trump's first term approved roughly twice the new borrowing Biden's did, 8.4 trillion against 4.3, and 4.8 against 2.2 once you strip pandemic relief from both [8]. In fairness, the same budget office scores the 2025 tariffs as cutting deficits by 4 trillion, so the current president is the first Republican in the period to pay for part of his tax cut, by taxing his own voters at the register [9]. Both parties spend. One says the opposite while doing it.

**The economy.** Four recessions since 1986, in 1990, 2001, 2007 and 2020. All four began under Republican presidents [10]. Of the 51.5 million jobs added since 1989, about 50 million came under Democrats, and Trump is the first modern president to leave with fewer jobs than he started with [11][12]. Call it luck and timing, and much of it is. What you cannot do is run the claim backward. The party that sells itself as the better manager has no forty-year evidence for the sale.

**Wars.** Every war of the era that put an army on the ground and kept it there was started by a Republican president: the Gulf, Afghanistan, Iraq, and, on this tracker's record, Iran. The post-9/11 wars cost about 8 trillion dollars and more than 7,000 American service members [13][14]. Democrats voted for Iraq, funded it, and extended it. Their own wars were fought from the air and ended in months. Bad, and not the same size or the same bill.

**Policy.** Forty years ago the party had proposals you could argue with. Then its own senators killed its president's immigration bill in 2007, 37 to 12 against [15]. In 2020 it adopted no platform at all, only a resolution to support whatever the president wanted [16]. After seven years of promising a health plan, the answer in 2024 was "concepts of a plan" [17]. What replaced proposals was a culprit, and the culprit chosen is the one the data clears: the largest arrest study in Texas found citizens born here were more than twice as likely as the undocumented to be arrested for violent crime [18]. Democrats blame billionaires, who can hire lawyers. That is the difference between a target and a scapegoat.

**Media.** Mainstream failures end in retractions and settlements inside a norm. Fox's ended in a 787.5 million dollar settlement after its own chairman testified under oath that his hosts "were endorsing" claims they knew were false [19]. The network fired its top host a week later and kept the rest [20]. Its lawyers had already won a separate case by arguing that no reasonable viewer takes that host as stating facts, and a federal judge agreed [21]. The daily product is subtraction: when researchers paid Fox viewers to watch CNN for a month, their factual beliefs moved, and drifted back within two months of going home to Fox [22].

**Science.** The party did not inherit denial. Reagan signed the ozone treaty and the elder Bush built the cap-and-trade program that ended acid rain [23][24]. Then a 2002 memo told the party to make "the lack of scientific certainty a primary issue," and by 2015 the Republican Party stood alone among nine conservative parties in the rich democracies in denying human-caused climate change [25][26]. It has now left the Paris agreement twice [27]. A party that had sight and sold it.

**The vote.** Every Democratic nominee who lost in these forty years conceded. In 2020 the other side lost more than sixty lawsuits, heard its own attorney general say there was no fraud "on a scale that could have effected a different outcome," and fired the official who called the election the most secure in American history [28][29][30]. It is still the party's position.

One party fails the way parties fail: bad bills, broken promises, captured incentives. The other has spent forty years failing the things that let you punish a party: the arithmetic, the record, the evidence, and finally the election. You can vote against the first kind. The second kind has been working on whether you get to.

## Short version

Both parties fail. Democrats deported at record rates, bombed Libya without Congress, gave Iraq 29 Senate votes, and signed the deregulation behind 2008. Grant all of it. Then look at the last forty years.

- **Money.** The deficit turned toward balance twice, both times by tax increase, one with zero Republican votes. Every big deficit-widening tax law was Republican. Trump's first term approved twice Biden's borrowing, pandemic relief excluded.
- **Economy.** Four recessions, all four starting under Republican presidents. About 50 million of the 51.5 million jobs added since 1989 came under Democrats. Luck explains some of it. It cannot explain it the other way.
- **Wars.** Every ground war with an occupation was Republican: the Gulf, Afghanistan, Iraq, Iran. Eight trillion dollars, over 7,000 dead.
- **Policy.** Its senators killed its own president's immigration bill. In 2020, no platform. In 2024, "concepts of a plan." Proposals were replaced by a scapegoat the data clears.
- **Media.** Fox paid 787.5 million dollars after its chairman swore its hosts endorsed claims they knew were false. Its lawyers won another case by arguing viewers should not take its top host as fact.
- **Science.** Reagan signed the ozone treaty. A 2002 memo told the party to sell uncertainty. By 2015 it was the only conservative party in nine rich democracies denying climate change.
- **The vote.** Every losing Democrat conceded. In 2020 the other side lost sixty lawsuits, was told by its own attorney general there was no fraud, fired the official who said the election was secure, and kept the claim.

One party fails the way parties fail. The other has failed the things that let you punish a party: the arithmetic, the record, the evidence, and the election itself. That is not a degree. It is a kind.

## Things not to say (they fail a fact-check)

- **"Republicans started more wars."** False over the long run: Democrats took the country into both world wars, Korea and Vietnam, and Obama into Libya. The defensible version is the 40-year one above: every ground war with an occupation since 1986 began under a Republican.
- **"Republicans cause recessions."** The pattern is real but the best study credits luck and oil shocks more than policy. Say "began under," not "caused." [NEEDS SOURCE: Blinder and Watson, 2016, American Economic Review]
- **"Fox is not news."** Fox has a news desk that has broken from the opinion side (the 2020 Arizona call). The defensible claim is about the ecosystem and the documented settlements.
- **"The right has no policy."** It has plenty (tax cuts, tariffs, Project 2025). The defensible claim is about which problems its policies address and how it campaigns.
