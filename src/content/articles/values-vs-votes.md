## Each topic in detail

### border security

**The value.** 2024 Republican platform: "We will end the Invasion at the Southern Border" ([source](https://www.presidency.ucsb.edu/documents/2024-republican-party-platform)) Adopted July 2024, after both votes

**The votes.**

- [Senate, 2024-02-07](https://www.senate.gov/legislative/LIS/roll_call_votes/vote1182/vote_118_2_00039.htm): Cloture on motion to proceed, H.R. 815 (Lankford-Murphy-Sinema border deal plus foreign aid). Rejected 49-50. R 4–44 (1 nv), D 43–5, I 2–1. R yes: Collins, Lankford, Murkowski, Romney. The page names only H.R. 815; FactCheck.org ties it to the border deal.
- [Senate, 2024-05-23](https://www.senate.gov/legislative/LIS/roll_call_votes/vote1182/vote_118_2_00182.htm): Cloture on motion to proceed, S. 4361 (Border Act, standalone). Rejected 43-50. R 1–44 (4 nv), D 41–4 (3 nv), I 1–2. R yes: Murkowski. Lankford, its author, voted no.

**Their stated reasons.**

- House GOP leadership (Johnson, Scalise, Emmer, Stefanik) (2024-02-05): "It is DEAD on arrival in the House." ([source](https://www.speaker.gov/2024/02/05/house-republican-leadership-statement-on-senate-immigration-bill/))
- Donald Trump, Las Vegas rally (2024-01-27): "Please blame it on me. Please." ([source](https://www.bostonglobe.com/2024/01/28/nation/trump-brags-about-efforts-stymie-border-talks-please-blame-it-me/))
- Donald Trump, Truth Social (quoted by Fox News) (2024-02-05): "Only a fool, or a Radical Left Democrat, would vote for this horrendous Border Bill" ([source](https://www.foxnews.com/politics/trump-blasts-horrendous-senate-border-deal-great-gift-democrats))
- Sen. James Lankford, to CNN, before the May vote (2024-05-21): "It's no longer a bill. It's a prop." ([source](https://www.cnn.com/2024/05/23/politics/senate-border-bill-vote/index.html))

**What complicates it.**

- Feb 8, 2024 the Senate proceeded to H.R. 815 67-32 (17 R yes) and on Feb 13 passed the aid without the border provisions, 70-29 (22 R yes).
- Some Democrats voted no from the left in both votes (Menendez called the deal an "outright betrayal").
- FactCheck.org rated Trump's "5,000 encounters" description wrong: emergency authority began at 4,000 a day.
- The factcheck: https://www.factcheck.org/2024/02/unraveling-misinformation-about-bipartisan-immigration-bill/

Confidence: high.

### IVF / family

**The value.** Donald Trump, Fox News town hall: "I'm the father of IVF" ([source](https://www.nbcnews.com/politics/2024-election/trump-says-father-ivf-recently-learned-rcna175940)) Aired Oct 16, 2024; the campaign later called it a joke. Platform: "supporting mothers and policies that advance Prenatal Care, access to Birth Control, and IVF (fertility treatments)."

**The votes.**

- [Senate, 2024-06-13](https://www.senate.gov/legislative/LIS/roll_call_votes/vote1182/vote_118_2_00197.htm): Cloture on motion to proceed, S. 4445 Right to IVF Act. Rejected 48-47. R 2–46 (1 nv), D 44–1 (2 nv), I 2–0 (2 nv). R yes: Collins, Murkowski. Schumer no for procedural reasons.
- [Senate, 2024-09-17](https://www.senate.gov/legislative/LIS/roll_call_votes/vote1182/vote_118_2_00242.htm): Same, upon reconsideration (after the platform was adopted). Rejected 51-44. R 2–44 (3 nv).

**Their stated reasons.**

- Sen. John Cornyn (2024-06-13): "Why should we vote for a bill that fixes a non-existent problem? There's not a problem. There's no restrictions on IVF, nor should there be." ([source](https://www.cnn.com/2024/06/13/politics/senate-ivf-bill-vote/index.html))
- Sen. John Thune (2024-09-17): "Republicans support IVF, full stop" ([source](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/senate-ivf-vote-republicans-trump/))

**What complicates it.**

- Republicans offered the Britt-Cruz IVF Protection Act; Democrats blocked it by objection June 12, 2024.
- The Democratic bill included an insurance-coverage mandate, the "overreach" Republicans cited.

Confidence: high.

## What this proves, and what it doesn't

Many of these were procedural votes, not final passage. Almost every no came with a reason, and some are real
arguments: the gun provisions, the insurance mandates, the tie to Democrats' larger bills. Some Republicans were
decisive in passing these laws: 15 Republican senators passed the gun bill, 13 House Republicans carried the
infrastructure law, and Senate Republicans split evenly on the Violence Against Women Act in 2013.

The pattern survives that. When a bill that advances a value Republicans campaign on arrives with Democrats' names on
it, most of them vote no, and the reason given is almost never the value itself. It is procedure, timing, an attached
provision, or a warning from Trump. The border bill is the cleanest case: their own negotiator wrote it, and the man
running for president asked them to kill it. The veterans' vote is the same story in miniature: 25 senators voted yes,
then no on the same text, then yes again after the outcry.

The one-line version: their stated values fold whenever they collide with party advantage, and the voting record lets
anyone watch it happen, one roll call at a time.
