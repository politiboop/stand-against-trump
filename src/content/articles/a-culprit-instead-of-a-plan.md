Whether Republicans "care" about climate, war, health care, education and equal rights is a question about what is in people's heads, and no record can settle it. What the record can settle is what the party spends its money and its words on, how large the problems it targets actually are, and what it did with the big problems once it had power. On all three, the answer points the same way.

This is the case, with the evidence against it stated at the end.

## What they campaign on

**The ads.** In October 2024, the Trump campaign spent more than $19 million on two ads about transgender people, aired nearly 55,000 times since October 1, CBS News reported. Co-campaign manager Chris LaCivita explained why: "It's the last thing on Earth they want to talk about," he said of Democrats. "So we'll talk about it for them." [1] Over one five-week stretch, the campaign's ads whose main subject was transgender issues cost about $29 million; ads mainly about the economy cost about $5 million [2]. For two weeks in late September and early October, gender identity came up in 48.2 percent of the Trump campaign's TV airings, by the Wesleyan Media Project's count [3].

Over the whole cycle, Republicans spent $222 million on ads attacking transgender and LGBTQ rights; the Trump campaign's "Kamala is for they/them" ads alone cost more than $46 million [4]. In the 2026 midterms, Republican advertisers spent more than $60 million on more than 190 TV ads about transgender policies by late September [5].

**What voters said mattered.** In Gallup's September 2024 poll, 52 percent of registered voters called the economy extremely important to their vote. Transgender rights came last of every issue tested, at 18 percent [6]. A CNN poll in September 2026 found the same order: the economy at 68 percent, transgender issues last of 11 at 25 percent [7].

**How big the target is.** Asked at a December 2024 Senate hearing how many of the NCAA's 510,000 college athletes are transgender, the association's president, Charlie Baker, answered, "Less than 10." [8] Georgia's 2024 audit of its voter rolls found 20 noncitizens among 8.2 million registered voters [9].

**The platform.** The 2024 Republican platform is short, about 5,400 words against the Democrats' 43,000, so raw counts favor the Democrats. Some words, though, do not appear in it at all [10][11]:

| Word | Republican platform | Democratic platform |
|---|---|---|
| climate | 0 | 81 |
| Medicaid | 0 | 26 |
| Ukraine | 0 | 22 |
| "illegal" | 16 | 11 |
| "alien" or "aliens" | 9 | 0 |
| "invasion" or "invade" | 7 | 4 |

On energy, the Republican platform says, "We will DRILL, BABY, DRILL." On health care, it offers one paragraph that opens, "Healthcare and prescription drug costs are out of control," and promises "Transparency" and "Choice and Competition." On education: "We are going to close the Department of Education in Washington, D.C. and send it back to the States." [10]

## The culprit, in their own words

In place of a plan, a culprit. At the September 2024 debate, Trump said, "In Springfield, they're eating the dogs. The people that came in. They're eating the cats. They're eating -- they're eating the pets of the people that live there." The city manager had already told ABC News "there have been no credible reports of specific claims of pets being harmed, injured or abused by individuals within the immigrant community." [12] Five days later JD Vance said on CNN, "If I have to create stories so that the American media actually pays attention to the suffering of the American people, then that's what I'm going to do, Dana." [13] Ohio's Republican governor, Mike DeWine, called the claim "a piece of garbage that was simply not true." [14] Springfield got dozens of bomb threats at schools, grocery stores and government buildings [15].

The year before, at a New Hampshire rally, Trump said of immigrants, "They're poisoning the blood of our country." [16]

In office, the pattern continued with the government's own numbers:
- **"Worst of the worst."** By ICE's own data, presented to Congress in April 2026, 176,000 of 457,000 arrests, 39 percent, involved people with no criminal record; about 14 percent had violent records, by an internal DHS document reported by CBS News [17][49].
- **Noncitizen voters.** DHS claimed in July 2026 that more than 250,000 noncitizens were registered in four states. Snopes rated the claim false [18].
- **Who gets in.** The 2027 refugee ceiling is 17,500, down from 125,000 in 2025, and the notice to Congress says admissions will "primarily be Afrikaners from South Africa." [19]
- **A judge's finding.** In July 2026, a federal judge blocked a freeze on green cards and work permits for immigrants from countries including Nigeria and Venezuela, writing that "This general hostility to immigration contrasts with an apparent interest in and preference for the migration of white people." [20]

## What they did with the big problems

The administration does state policies on these issues, and they deserve to be quoted. On energy: "It shall be the policy of my Administration to make America energy dominant." [21] On education: return it "to the States." [22] The record of what those policies did:

**Climate.** The United States' withdrawal from the Paris Agreement took effect January 27, 2026 [23]. In January 2026 the president ordered withdrawal from 66 international organizations, the U.N. climate treaty and its scientific panel among them [24]. In February, the EPA repealed the finding that greenhouse gases endanger public health, calling it the "single largest deregulatory action in U.S. history." [25] The 2025 budget law ended or cut tax credits for electric vehicles, home clean energy, and wind and solar power worth roughly half a trillion dollars over ten years, by the Joint Committee on Taxation's estimates [26]. All of the roughly 400 authors of the congressionally mandated National Climate Assessment were dismissed in April 2025, and the assessments were removed from federal websites [27][28].

**Health care.** Before the election, asked for his health plan, Trump said, "I have concepts of a plan. I'm not President right now." [29] In office, the budget law's Medicaid provisions "will increase the number of people without health insurance by 7.5 million in 2034," the Congressional Budget Office estimates [30]. The enhanced Affordable Care Act credits expired at the end of 2025. What enrollees paid rose 58 percent on average, and enrollment fell from 22.1 million to 19.2 million [31][32]. The House passed a three-year extension in January 2026 only by going around Speaker Johnson with a discharge petition [33].

**Education.** The Education Department's staff went from 4,133 to 2,183 [34]. The Supreme Court let the layoffs proceed in July 2025 [35], and the department began moving its largest programs to other agencies [36]. On July 1, 2025, the administration held back about $6.8 billion in funding for schools [37].

**War and foreign policy.** The Iran war began on February 28, 2026 without a declaration or a vote of Congress [38]. By the Congressional Budget Office's count it cost $38.1 billion through August 1, and "DoD did not respond to CBO's requests for information." The office estimates the United States has used between half and two-thirds of its missile-defense interceptors [39]. The Pentagon's own count shows at least 19 American dead [40]. Abroad, USAID closed on July 1, 2025 [41]; a Lancet study projects the cuts could cause more than 14 million additional deaths by 2030 unless reversed [42].

**Equal rights.** An executive order revoked the 1965 order that required federal contractors to practice equal employment opportunity [43]. About 250 attorneys, roughly 70 percent, left the Justice Department's Civil Rights Division by May 2025 [44]. In September 2026 the department announced it had ended more than 50 school desegregation cases [45]. The Supreme Court let the ban on transgender troops take effect [46].

## The case against this argument

The fair version has to include what cuts against it.

- **Most of the money went to the economy.** Across the whole 2024 campaign, the Wesleyan Media Project found both candidates "placed the most emphasis on bread-and-butter issues like the economy and taxes," and pro-Trump ads focused on transgender policy only "for a brief span." [3] The $46 million in "they/them" ads was about 3 percent of the roughly $1.4 billion in pro-Trump presidential advertising [4][47].
- **Immigration was not a fringe issue.** It was voters' second concern in 2024, ahead of health care and education, and first among Trump's voters [6][48]. The claim holds for the transgender ads and the false claims about immigrants, not for talking about immigration.
- **Democrats run identity campaigns too.** Their 2024 platform mentions LGBTQI people 37 times and promises to "end violence against transgender Americans, especially Black and brown transgender women." [11]
- **Voters did not rank climate highly.** In Gallup's poll it came second to last, at 21 percent [6].
- **Some of this was not the administration alone.** The Supreme Court decided the transgender troops case, the frozen school money was later released, and Congress, not the president, would have to abolish the Education Department.

What survives is still enough. The platform is silent on climate, Medicaid and Ukraine. The largest single ad push of the campaign's final weeks went to a group that is a rounding error among college athletes. In office, the policy on the largest problems has been removal: of the climate treaty, the climate science, the health coverage and the civil-rights lawyers. Whether they care is a guess about motive. What the record shows is a party that treats the big problems as things to stop working on, and a small group of people as the thing to fight about.
