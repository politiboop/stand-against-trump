You are right about plenty. Both parties take the money. Obama removed people at a higher rate than any president before him, and he bombed Libya without asking Congress [1][2]. A majority of Senate Democrats, 29 of 50, voted for the Iraq war [3]. None of that makes the two parties the same. Here is what the record says when you ask the question directly.

**Money.** In forty years the deficit turned toward balance twice, and both times the tool was a tax increase. The first was the 1990 deal the elder Bush was pilloried for by his own party. The second was the 1993 act that passed without a single Republican vote and needed the vice president to break a tie in the Senate [4][5]. The surpluses of 1998 to 2001 followed [6]. The two largest tax laws of the period, in 2017 and 2025, were Republican: the Congressional Budget Office put the first at $1.854 trillion in added deficits over a decade even after counting its effect on growth, and the second at $3.4 trillion [7][8]. Trump's first term approved roughly twice the new borrowing Biden's did, $8.4 trillion against $4.3 trillion, and $4.8 trillion against $2.2 trillion with pandemic relief removed from both [9]. In fairness, the budget office scores the 2025 tariffs as cutting deficits by $4 trillion, so the current president is paying for part of his tax cut, by taxing his own voters at the register [10]. Both parties spend. One says the opposite while doing it.

**The economy.** There have been four recessions since 1986, starting in 1990, 2001, 2007 and 2020 [11]. All four began under Republican presidents. Of the 51.5 million jobs added since 1989, about 50 million came under Democrats, and Trump became the first modern president to leave office with fewer jobs than when he started [12][13]. Much of that is luck and timing. What you cannot do is run the claim backward. The party that sells itself as the better manager has no forty-year evidence for the sale.

**Wars.** The two largest wars of the period, Afghanistan and Iraq, began under a Republican president, and so did the Iran war now underway. The post-9/11 wars cost about $8 trillion and more than 7,000 American service members [14][15]. Democrats voted for Iraq, funded it and extended it, and Obama bombed Libya without Congress. None of it is on the same scale.

**Policy.** Forty years ago the party had proposals you could argue with. In 2007 its own senators killed its president's immigration bill, 37 to 12 against [16]. In 2020 it adopted no platform at all, only a resolution to support whatever the president wanted [17]. After seven years of promising a health plan, the answer in 2024 was "concepts of a plan" [18]. What replaced proposals was a culprit, and the culprit chosen is the one the data clears. The largest arrest study in Texas found that citizens born here were more than twice as likely as undocumented immigrants to be arrested for violent crime [19]. Democrats blame billionaires, who can hire lawyers. That is the difference between a target and a scapegoat.

**Media.** Mainstream failures end in retractions and settlements, inside a norm. Fox's ended in a $787.5 million settlement after its own chairman acknowledged under oath that some of its hosts were endorsing claims of a stolen election [20]. The network parted ways with its top host a week later [21]. Its lawyers had already won a separate case by arguing that no reasonable viewer takes that host as stating facts, and a federal judge agreed [22]. When researchers paid Fox viewers to watch CNN for a month, their factual beliefs moved, and they drifted back within two months of returning to Fox [23].

**Science.** The party did not inherit denial. Reagan signed the ozone treaty, and the elder Bush built the cap-and-trade program that cut acid rain [24][25]. Then a 2002 memo told the party to make "the lack of scientific certainty a primary issue," and by 2015 the Republican Party stood alone among nine conservative parties in wealthy democracies in denying human-caused climate change [26][27]. It has now left the Paris agreement twice [28].

**The vote.** In 2020 Trump and his allies lost more than sixty lawsuits over the election. His own attorney general said federal investigators had found no fraud on a scale that could have changed the outcome, and Trump fired the official whose agency called the election the most secure in American history [29][30][31].

One party fails the way parties fail: bad bills, broken promises, captured incentives. The other has spent forty years failing the things that let you punish a party: the arithmetic, the record, the evidence, and finally the election. You can vote against the first kind. The second kind has been working on whether you get to.

The vote-by-vote record is in [What They Say, How They Vote](/values-vs-votes) and [Every Epstein Files Vote](/epstein-votes).

## The short version

Both parties fail. Democrats deported at record rates, bombed Libya without Congress and gave the Iraq war 29 Senate votes. Grant all of it. Then look at the last forty years.

- **Money.** The deficit turned toward balance twice, both times by tax increase, one with zero Republican votes. The two largest tax laws, in 2017 and 2025, were Republican. Trump's first term approved twice Biden's borrowing, pandemic relief excluded.
- **The economy.** Four recessions, all four starting under Republican presidents. About 50 million of the 51.5 million jobs added since 1989 came under Democrats.
- **Wars.** Afghanistan, Iraq and now Iran: about $8 trillion for the first two and more than 7,000 American dead.
- **Policy.** Its senators killed its own president's immigration bill. In 2020, no platform. In 2024, "concepts of a plan."
- **Media.** Fox paid $787.5 million after its chairman acknowledged hosts endorsed false claims. Its lawyers won another case by arguing viewers should not take its top host as stating facts.
- **Science.** Reagan signed the ozone treaty. A 2002 memo told the party to sell uncertainty. By 2015 it was the only conservative party of nine in wealthy democracies denying climate change.
- **The vote.** It lost sixty lawsuits, was told by its own attorney general there was no outcome-changing fraud, and fired the official who said the election was secure.
